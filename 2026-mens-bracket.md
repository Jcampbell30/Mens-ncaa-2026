# 2026 NCAA Men's Basketball Tournament — Complete Bracket Predictions

## Methodology

This bracket was constructed using a multi-source analytical framework:

1. **Baseline**: Official 2026 NCAA Tournament bracket seedings and matchups (Selection Sunday bracket)
2. **Predictive Metrics**: KenPom-style offensive/defensive efficiency ratings, adjusted tempo, Torvik T-Rank projections, and Haslametrics momentum indicators
3. **Consensus Projections**: Bracket Matrix aggregate data, ESPN (Lunardi), CBS (Palm), and The Athletic expert analysis
4. **Committee Signals**: NCAA NET rankings, Quadrant 1 records, strength of schedule

**Weighting (when sources conflict):**
1. Aggregated analytics models (KenPom, Torvik) — highest priority
2. Consensus projections (Bracket Matrix)
3. Expert analysts (Lunardi, Palm, Athletic writers)
4. Narrative factors (injuries, coaching, experience)

## Final Four Justification

### (1) Duke — East Region Champion
Duke's 32-2 record represents one of the program's all-time great seasons. Their adjusted offensive efficiency (top-5 nationally) and adjusted defensive efficiency (top-3 nationally) create an efficiency margin that only Arizona can match. Their guard depth allows them to control pace and create mismatches. Duke's coaching lineage and tournament pedigree — 6 national championships — translate to peak performance in high-pressure March environments. They navigated UConn (2 seed) in the Elite Eight, proving they can beat the very best.

### (1) Arizona — West Region Champion
Arizona's 32-2 record mirrors Duke's dominance. Under Tommy Lloyd, Arizona plays an up-tempo, efficient offense that creates transition opportunities and open three-point looks. Their defensive versatility and frontcourt depth powered them through a Gonzaga (30-3) matchup in the Elite Eight. Arizona's adjusted offensive efficiency ranks top-3 nationally, and their ability to control tempo on both ends makes them a legitimate championship contender.

### (2) Houston — South Region Champion
Houston's elite defensive efficiency — consistently top-3 nationally under Kelvin Sampson — carried them through the South Region, including a signature upset of 1-seed Florida in the Elite Eight. Their 28-6 record as a 2 seed masked a team that performed at 1-seed analytical levels. Houston's defensive rebounding rate, shot-blocking, and perimeter pressure created problems for every opponent. Sampson's tournament pedigree (multiple Final Four appearances) reinforced their mental toughness in March.

### (1) Michigan — Midwest Region Champion
Michigan's 31-3 record is the second-best in the field. Their balanced offensive attack (no single-player dependency) and disciplined defense created a consistency that carried them through a tough Midwest bracket. Michigan's ability to win in different styles — up-tempo or grind-it-out — made them adaptable against varying opponents. Their depth and offensive rebounding were decisive factors in the Elite Eight win over Iowa State.

## Champion Justification: Duke

### Efficiency Metrics
- Duke's adjusted efficiency margin (offensive efficiency minus defensive efficiency) is the best or second-best in the nation, a metric that historically correlates most strongly with tournament success
- Their offensive efficiency ranks top-5 nationally, driven by elite three-point shooting percentage and free-throw rate
- Their defensive efficiency ranks top-3 nationally, with low opponent effective field goal percentage and strong defensive rebounding

### Matchup Advantages
- Duke's guard depth allows them to match up against any backcourt in the country
- Their defensive versatility (ability to switch 1-through-4) neutralizes ball-screen-heavy offenses like Arizona's and Michigan's
- Duke's balanced scoring (no single player accounts for >25% of points) makes them resistant to game-planning

### Historical Trends
- Teams with 30+ wins and fewer than 3 losses win the national championship at an elevated rate
- Duke's program has won 6 national championships, more than any school except UCLA's 11 — tournament experience matters at the coaching and program level
- The 1 overall seed (or equivalent) wins the championship approximately 25% of the time, far exceeding any other single seed line

### Path to the Title
Duke's path required beating UConn (2 seed, 29-5) in the Elite Eight, Arizona (1 seed, 32-2) in the Final Four, and Michigan (1 seed, 31-3) in the Championship. This gauntlet validates Duke as a worthy champion — they beat the three most dangerous remaining teams in sequence.
